悪い少年の物語

昔、ジムという名の悪い少年がいた――もっとも、よく見れば、日曜学校の本に出てくる悪い少年は、ほとんどいつもジェイムズという名だと分かるだろう。妙なことだが、この少年はたしかにジムと呼ばれていた。
Once there was a bad little boy whose name was Jim—though, if you will notice, you will find that bad little boys are nearly always called James in your Sunday school books. It was strange, but still it was true that this one was called Jim.
それに彼には病気の母親もいなかった――信心深くて結核を患い、墓に横たわって休めたらどんなにうれしいかと思いながらも、息子への強い愛と、自分が死んだあと世間がこの子に冷たく厳しく当たりはしないかという心配のため、それができないような母親は。日曜学校の本に出てくる悪い少年はたいていジェイムズといい、病気の母親がいる。母親は「これから私は眠ります」などという祈りを教え、甘く悲しげな声で歌って眠らせ、おやすみのキスをし、ベッドのそばにひざまずいて泣く。だが、この少年の場合は違った。名前はジムで、母親はどこも悪くなかった――結核でも何でもない。むしろ太っていたし、信心深くもなかった。そのうえジムのことを心配してもいなかった。
He didn't have any sick mother either—a sick mother who was pious and had the consumption, and would be glad to lie down in the grave and be at rest but for the strong love she bore her boy, and the anxiety she felt that the world might be harsh and cold towards him when she was gone. Most bad boys in the Sunday books are named James, and have sick mothers, who teach them to say "Now, I lay me down," etc., and sing them to sleep with sweet, plaintive voices, and then kiss them good night, and kneel down by the bedside and weep. But it was different with this fellow. He was named Jim, and there wasn't anything the matter with his mother—no consumption, nor anything of that kind. She was rather stout than otherwise, and she was not pious; moreover, she was not anxious on Jim's account.
母親は、あの子が首を折ってもたいした損ではないと言った。いつもジムを叩いて眠らせ、キスしておやすみを言うことはなかった。それどころか、立ち去るときには耳を張った。
She said if he were to break his neck it wouldn't be much loss. She always spanked Jim to sleep, and she never kissed him good night; on the contrary, she boxed his ears when she was ready to leave him.
あるとき、この悪い少年は食品庫の鍵を盗み、中へ忍び込んでジャムを好きなだけ食べ、母親に違いが分からないよう、容器をタールで満たした。だが突然、恐ろしい感情に襲われることはなかったし、「母に背くのは正しいことだろうか？　これは罪ではないか？　やさしい善良な母親のジャムをむさぼる悪い少年は、死んだらどこへ行くのだろう？」と何かが囁くようにも思えなかった。そして一人ひざまずいて、もう決して悪いことをしないと誓い、明るく幸福な心で立ち上がって、母親にすべてを告白して許しを乞い、誇りと感謝の涙を浮かべた母親から祝福される、ということもなかった。
Once this little bad boy stole the key of the pantry, and slipped in there and helped himself to some jam, and filled up the vessel with tar, so that his mother would never know the difference; but all at once a terrible feeling didn't come over him, and something didn't seem to whisper to him, "Is it right to disobey my mother? Isn't it sinful to do this? Where do bad little boys go who gobble up their good kind mother's jam?" and then he didn't kneel down all alone and promise never to be wicked any more, and rise up with a light, happy heart, and go and tell his mother all about it, and beg her forgiveness, and be blessed by her with tears of pride and thankfulness in her eyes.
いや、ほかの本の悪い少年ならそうなるのだが、奇妙なことに、このジムの場合は違った。彼はジャムを食べ、罪深く下品な言い方で「最高だ」と言った。タールを入れたときにも「最高だ」と言い、笑って、見つけたら「あのばばあ、かんかんになって鼻息を荒くするぞ」と言った。実際に見つかると、何も知らないと言い張り、母親にひどく鞭で打たれ、自分で泣いた。この少年に関することは何もかも変だった――本の中の悪いジェイムズたちとは、何もかも違う結末になった。
No; that is the way with all other bad boys in the books; but it happened otherwise with this Jim, strangely enough. He ate that jam, and said it was bully, in his sinful, vulgar way; and he put in the tar, and said that was bully also, and laughed, and observed "that the old woman would get up and snort" when she found it out; and when she did find it out, he denied knowing anything about it, and she whipped him severely, and he did the crying himself. Everything about this boy was curious—everything turned out differently with him from the way it does to the bad Jameses in the books.
あるとき彼は、りんごを盗むためエイコーン農夫の木に登った。ところが枝は折れず、落ちて腕を折ることも、農夫の大きな犬に引き裂かれることも、その後何週間も病床で衰弱し、悔い改めて善い少年になることもなかった。とんでもない。欲しいだけりんごを盗み、無事に降りてきた。犬に対する用意もできていて、襲いかかってきたところを煉瓦で横倒しにした。じつに奇妙だった――大理石模様の背表紙がついた、燕尾服と釣鐘形の帽子、丈の短いズボンをはいた男や、胴が脇の下にあり、輪骨を入れていない服の女の絵が載った、おとなしい小さな本では、こんなことは一度も起きなかった。日曜学校のどの本にも、こんな話はなかった。
Once he climbed up in Farmer Acorn's apple-tree to steal apples, and the limb didn't break, and he didn't fall and break his arm, and get torn by the farmer's great dog, and then languish on a sick bed for weeks, and repent and become good. Oh, no; he stole as many apples as he wanted, and came down all right; and he was all ready for the dog, too, and knocked him endways with a brick when he came to tear him. It was very strange—nothing like it ever happened in those mild little books with marbled backs, and with pictures in them of men with swallow-tailed coats and bell-crowned hats, and pantaloons that are short in the legs, and women with the waists of their dresses under their arms, and no hoops on. Nothing like it in any of the Sunday school books.
あるとき彼は先生の小刀を盗んだ。見つかって鞭で打たれるのが怖くなり、ジョージ・ウィルソンの帽子に忍ばせた――貧しい未亡人ウィルソンの息子で、道徳的な少年、村の善い少年、いつも母親に従い、決して嘘をつかず、勉強が好きで、日曜学校に夢中の少年である。帽子から小刀が落ち、かわいそうなジョージが罪を自覚したようにうつむいて赤くなり、心を痛めた先生が盗みの罪をなじり、震える肩へ鞭を振り下ろそうとしたその瞬間――白髪の、いかにもありそうにない治安判事が突然現れて格好をつけ、「この気高い少年を許したまえ――おびえる犯人はそこにいる！　休み時間に学校の戸口を通り、誰にも見られず盗みを目撃したのだ！」と言うことはなかった。
Once he stole the teacher's pen-knife, and, when he was afraid it would be found out and he would get whipped, he slipped it into George Wilson's cap—poor widow Wilson's son, the moral boy, the good little boy of the village, who always obeyed his mother, and never told an untruth, and was fond of his lessons, and infatuated with Sunday school. And when the knife dropped from the cap, and poor George hung his head and blushed, as if in conscious guilt, and the grieved teacher charged the theft upon him, and was just in the very act of bringing the switch down upon his trembling shoulders, a white-haired, improbable justice of the peace did not suddenly appear in their midst, and strike an attitude and say, "Spare this noble boy—there stands the cowering culprit! I was passing the school door at recess, and unseen myself, I saw the theft committed!"
だからジムが鞭で打たれることも、老判事が涙ぐむ学校の一同に説教をし、ジョージの手を取って、こんな少年は出世するに値すると言い、家に来て暮らし、事務所の掃除、火起こし、使い走り、薪割り、法律の勉強、妻の家事の手伝いをし、そのほかの時間はすべて遊び、月四十セントをもらって幸福に暮らせ、と言うこともなかった。いや、本ならそうなったはずだが、ジムの場合は違った。余計なお節介をする判事の老いぼれは現れず、模範少年ジョージが鞭で打たれた。ジムは喜んだ。というのも、ジムは道徳的な少年が嫌いだったからだ。ジムは「へなちょこどもは大嫌いだ」と言った。これが、この悪く、顧みられない少年の粗野な言葉遣いだった。
And then Jim didn't get whaled, and the venerable justice didn't read the tearful school a homily, and take George by the hand and say such a boy deserved to be exalted, and then tell him to come and make his home with him, and sweep out the office, and make fires, and run errands, and chop wood, and study law, and help his wife do household labours, and have all the balance of the time to play, and get forty cents a month, and be happy. No; it would have happened that way in the books, but it didn't happen that way to Jim. No meddling old clam of a justice dropped in to make trouble, and so the model boy George got thrashed, and Jim was glad of it because, you know, Jim hated moral boys. Jim said he was "down on them milksops." Such was the coarse language of this bad, neglected boy.
しかしジムに起きた中で最も奇妙なのは、日曜日に舟遊びへ行ったのに溺れなかったことと、別の日曜日に釣りをして嵐に遭ったのに、雷に打たれなかったことだ。今から次のクリスマスまで日曜学校の本を探し続けても、こんなことは見つからないだろう。とんでもない。日曜に舟へ乗る悪い少年は例外なく溺れ、日曜に釣りをして嵐に遭う悪い少年は、必ず雷に打たれる。悪い少年を乗せた舟は日曜には必ず転覆し、悪い少年が安息日に釣りへ行けば、必ず嵐になる。このジムがどうやって助かったのか、私には謎だ。
But the strangest thing that ever happened to Jim was the time he went boating on Sunday, and didn't get drowned, and that other time that he got caught out in the storm when he was fishing on Sunday, and didn't get struck by lightning. Why, you might look, and look, all through the Sunday school books from now till next Christmas, and you would never come across anything like this. Oh no; you would find that all the bad boys who go boating on Sunday invariably get drowned; and all the bad boys who get caught out in storms when they are fishing on Sunday, infallibly get struck by lightning. Boats with bad boys in them always upset on Sunday, and it always storms when bad boys go fishing on the Sabbath. How this Jim ever escaped is a mystery to me.
このジムは魔法に守られた命を持っていた――きっとそうに違いない。何ものも彼を傷つけられなかった。見世物小屋の象に噛み煙草をやっても、象は鼻で頭のてっぺんを吹き飛ばさなかった。食器棚でペパーミント精を探り回っても、間違えて硝酸を飲むことはなかった。父親の銃を盗み、安息日に狩りへ行っても、指を三、四本撃ち落とさなかった。腹を立てて妹のこめかみを拳で殴っても、妹が長い夏の日々を苦痛の中で衰え、唇に甘い許しの言葉を残して死に、砕けた彼の心の苦悩を倍加させることはなかった。いや、妹は治った。最後には家出して船乗りになったが、帰ってみると世に一人残され、愛する者は静かな墓地に眠り、蔦のからむ少年時代の家は崩れて朽ちていた、ということもなかった。とんでもない。彼はひどく酔って帰り、真っ先に留置場へ入れられた。
This Jim bore a charmed life—that must have been the way of it. Nothing could hurt him. He even gave the elephant in the menagerie a plug of tobacco, and the elephant didn't knock the top of his head off with his trunk. He browsed around the cupboard after essence of peppermint, and didn't make a mistake and drink aqua fortis. He stole his father's gun and went hunting on the Sabbath, and didn't shoot three or four of his fingers off. He struck his little sister on the temple with his fist when he was angry, and she didn't linger in pain through long summer days; and die with sweet words of forgiveness upon her lips that redoubled the anguish of his breaking heart. No; she got over it. He ran off and went to sea at last, and didn't come back and find himself sad and alone in the world, his loved ones sleeping in the quiet churchyard, and the vine-embowered home of his boyhood tumbled down and gone to decay. Ah, no; he came home as drunk as a piper, and got into the station-house the first thing.
彼は大人になって結婚し、大勢の子どもを育て、ある晩その全員の頭を斧で割った。あらゆる詐欺と悪事で金持ちになり、今では故郷の村で最もとんでもなく邪悪な悪党であり、万人から尊敬され、州議会の議員になっている。
And he grew up and married, and raised a large family, and brained them all with an axe one night, and got wealthy by all manner of cheating and rascality; and now he is the infernalest wickedest scoundrel in his native village, and is universally respected, and belongs to the Legislature.
このように、日曜学校の本に出てくる悪いジェイムズの中に、魔法に守られた、この罪深いジムほど幸運に恵まれた者は一人もいなかったのである。
So you see there never was a bad James in the Sunday school books that had such a streak of luck as this sinful Jim with the charmed life.
